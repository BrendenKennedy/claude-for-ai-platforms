# NIST SP 800-63 — Digital Identity Guidelines

**Identity:** NIST · **Revision 4, final July 2025** (after a four-year process, two public drafts,
~6,000 comments) · https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/
**Verified:** 2026-07-25. Rev. 4 final publication in July 2025 confirmed; the Rev. 4 initial public
draft entry at CSRC is marked withdrawn because it was superseded by the final.
**Canon citing it:** `identity-and-access.md` (I1, I2, I5, I9)

> The reference standard for *how strong* an authentication needs to be, expressed as assurance
> levels rather than a list of mechanisms. We carry it because "use MFA" is not a specification and
> AAL2/AAL3 is, and because Rev. 4 finally caught the standard up to passkeys.

## The structure

Four volumes, and three independent assurance dimensions:

| Volume | Covers |
|---|---|
| 800-63-4 | The framework, risk-based selection of assurance levels |
| 800-63A-4 | Identity proofing and enrollment — **IAL** |
| 800-63B-4 | Authentication and authenticator management — **AAL** |
| 800-63C-4 | Federation and assertions — **FAL** |

| Dimension | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IAL** (proofing) | Self-asserted | Remote or in-person, evidence-based | In-person or supervised remote |
| **AAL** (authentication) | Single factor | **Two factors, replay-resistant** | Hardware-based, **phishing-resistant**, verifier-impersonation-resistant |
| **FAL** (federation) | Bearer assertion | Holder-of-key or signed assertion | Assertion bound to a cryptographic key the subscriber holds |

The three are chosen independently from a risk assessment — a system can require AAL2 with IAL1.

### What Rev. 4 changed

Modernised around risk-based evaluation and phishing-resistant MFA; added first-class support for
**syncable authenticators** (synced passkeys) and **subscriber-controlled wallets** in the federation
model; expanded fraud requirements around identity proofing; and added continuous-evaluation metrics.
The practical headline: passkeys are now a recognised authenticator with defined properties, rather
than something you argued about.

## What we adopt

- **AAL2 as the floor for any human access to a platform we build; AAL3 (phishing-resistant) for
  administrative access** — cluster admin, cloud console, production secrets, CI configuration.
  Canon `I2` states it. SMS OTP does not meet the bar for administrative access.
- **Phishing resistance as the property that matters**, not the factor count. WebAuthn/FIDO2 and
  passkeys qualify; TOTP does not resist a real-time relay.
- **The IAL/AAL/FAL separation** as the vocabulary for `/threat-model` when a system has human users
  — it prevents the common conflation of "we verified who they are" with "we authenticated them
  strongly."
- **Rev. 4's syncable-authenticator treatment** as the basis for accepting passkeys without a
  bespoke risk argument each time.

## What we leave

- **Federal identity-proofing procedure.** IAL2/IAL3 evidence collection is a heavy, jurisdiction-
  specific process; unless a project is doing regulated identity proofing, IAL1 plus an existing
  enterprise IdP is the honest position.
- **The full authenticator-type catalogue.** Referenced, not restated.
- **Conformance claims.** We adopt the assurance-level vocabulary as a design target;
  `compliance-crosswalk.md` maps to it and asserts nothing.

## Gotcha

**800-63 is about *human* identity.** It has essentially nothing to say about workload identity —
which, in a Kubernetes-based AI platform, is most of the identities in the system. For those, go to
`spiffe-spire.md` and `oauth-oidc.md`; canon `I3` (short-lived, platform-issued workload credentials)
is deliberately sourced from there rather than here. Applying AAL levels to a service account is a
category error.
