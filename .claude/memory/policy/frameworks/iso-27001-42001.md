# ISO/IEC 27001 and 42001 — management-system standards

**Identity:** ISO/IEC · **27001:2022** (information security management) · **42001:2023** (AI
management system, published December 2023) · https://www.iso.org/standard/27001 ·
https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html
**Verified:** 2026-07-25. 27001:2022 and 42001:2023 confirmed current. Major AI providers began
certifying against 42001 from late 2024 (AWS Nov 2024, Anthropic Jan 2025, Microsoft Jul 2025),
which is a reasonable proxy for the standard having become the expected one.
**Canon citing it:** `compliance-crosswalk.md` (mapping target only)

> Two certifiable management-system standards: 27001 for information security, 42001 for AI
> specifically. We carry them as **crosswalk targets** because they are what enterprise customers
> ask about, and because 42001 is currently the most direct route to demonstrating AI governance to a
> third party.

## ISO/IEC 27001:2022

An **Information Security Management System** standard: it certifies that you have a functioning
process for managing information security risk, not that any particular control is implemented.
Clauses 4–10 define the management system (context, leadership, planning, support, operation,
evaluation, improvement); **Annex A** lists 93 controls in four themes — Organizational (37), People
(8), Physical (14), Technological (34) — which the 2022 revision restructured from the previous 114
across 14 domains.

The certifiable object is the *system*: scope, risk assessment, Statement of Applicability,
management review, internal audit, continual improvement.

## ISO/IEC 42001:2023

The **AI Management System** standard, structurally parallel to 27001 and designed to be integrated
with it. It addresses AI-specific governance: AI policy, roles, impact assessment on individuals and
society, the AI system lifecycle, data management for AI, and third-party/supplier relationships for
AI components.

Its practical significance is the mapping to regulation. 42001 aligns with core EU AI Act
obligations — risk management (Art. 9), data governance (Art. 10), technical documentation (Art. 11),
record-keeping (Art. 12), transparency (Art. 13), human oversight (Art. 14), and quality management
(Art. 17) — which makes it a reasonable scaffold for AI Act readiness.

**With the caveat that matters:** 42001 is a voluntary management-system standard and the AI Act is
law. Certification supports compliance; it does not substitute for the specific artifacts the
regulation requires (technical documentation, declaration of conformity, CE marking, post-market
monitoring). See `eu-ai-act.md`.

## What we adopt

- **Annex A control references and 42001 clause references as crosswalk targets.**
- **42001's AI-lifecycle and impact-assessment framing** as a sanity check on `PROCESS.md`: if a
  phase gate has no place to record impact on affected people, that is a real gap, and it is the one
  a security-only framework would never surface.
- **The Statement-of-Applicability idea, informally.** `memory/process/control-coverage.md` is the
  same shape — what is implemented, what is not, and why — without the certification apparatus.

## What we leave

- **The certification process** entirely: audits, SoA formalities, management review minutes,
  internal audit programs. A repo cannot certify anything.
- **Clause-level management-system requirements** (leadership commitment, documented information
  control). Organisational, not architectural.
- **Any claim of certification or readiness.** Mapping to a control is not implementing it, and
  implementing it is not certifying it. The crosswalk header says this explicitly.

## Gotcha

**A management-system standard certifies the process, not the product.** A 27001-certified
organisation can ship an insecure service without contradiction, because what was audited was the
existence and operation of the risk-management process. This is not a criticism of the standards —
it is what they are for — but it means that neither certification is evidence that a specific system
is secure. That evidence comes from `cis-kubernetes.md`, `owasp-agentic-top10.md`, a red-team run,
and `control-coverage.md`.
