# NIST SP 800-53 — the control catalogue

**Identity:** NIST · **Revision 5** (September 2020, with updates through Rev. 5.1.1) ·
https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final
**Verified:** 2026-07-25. Rev. 5 remains the current revision.
**Canon citing it:** `compliance-crosswalk.md` (mapping target only)

> The exhaustive catalogue of security and privacy controls that US federal systems are assessed
> against, and the vocabulary FedRAMP, CMMC, and most enterprise vendor-security reviews inherit. We
> carry it purely as a **mapping target** — this repo asserts no 800-53 conformance and generates no
> control implementation statements.

## The structure

Controls are organised into families, each with a two-letter prefix, and cited as `AC-2`, `SC-7(3)`
where the parenthesised number is a control enhancement. The families that a platform project
actually touches:

| Family | Covers | Our nearest canon |
|---|---|---|
| **AC** Access Control | Account management, least privilege, separation of duties, remote access | `identity-and-access.md` (I1, I3, I4) · `platform-security.md` (P6) |
| **AU** Audit and Accountability | Audit events, content, storage, protection, review | `P9`, `AI12` |
| **CA** Assessment & Authorization | Control assessment, continuous monitoring | `/gate` · `control-coverage.md` |
| **CM** Configuration Management | Baseline configuration, least functionality, change control | `P1`–`P5` · `gitops` |
| **CP** Contingency Planning | Backup, recovery, alternate processing | `R3`, `R5` |
| **IA** Identification & Authentication | Authenticator management, MFA, device identification | `I1`, `I2`, `I3`, `I9` |
| **IR** Incident Response | Plan, handling, monitoring, reporting | `R7`, `R8` |
| **RA** Risk Assessment | Risk assessment, vulnerability scanning | `risk-register.md` · `C5` |
| **SA** System & Services Acquisition | SDLC, developer testing, supply chain | `supply-chain.md` (C1–C8) |
| **SC** System & Communications Protection | Boundary protection, cryptography in transit and at rest, isolation | `P5`, `P8`, `I7` |
| **SI** System & Information Integrity | Flaw remediation, malicious code, monitoring, input validation | `C5`, `AI1`, `AI5` |

Rev. 5's notable changes: privacy controls are integrated rather than separate, supply-chain risk
management got its own **SR** family, and controls became outcome-based rather than
impact-level-scoped.

## What we adopt

- **Control IDs as crosswalk targets only.** `compliance-crosswalk.md` maps our rule IDs → CSF
  Subcategory → 800-53 control, so a team facing a FedRAMP-flavoured questionnaire has a starting
  point rather than a blank page.
- **The family structure as a coverage check.** Reading the family list and asking "what do we have
  for CP? for IR?" is a cheap and effective way to find whole categories a threat model missed —
  which is most of the value a control catalogue offers a small team.

## What we leave

Nearly all of it, deliberately:

- **Baseline tailoring** (low/moderate/high) and the whole Risk Management Framework process.
- **Control implementation statements** and System Security Plans. Generating SSP prose from a
  scaffold would produce exactly the kind of unverified compliance artifact that makes assessments
  worthless.
- **Assessment procedures** (SP 800-53A).
- **Any conformance claim.** Mapping a rule to `AC-6` means "this rule is in the neighbourhood of
  least privilege," not "we satisfy AC-6." The crosswalk says so in its own header.

## Gotcha

**Do not let this catalogue drive engineering.** 800-53 Rev. 5 contains over a thousand controls and
enhancements; a small team that tries to work from it produces a spreadsheet instead of a secure
system. The prescriptive documents — `cis-kubernetes.md`, `k8s-pod-security-standards.md`,
`owasp-agentic-top10.md` — are what you build from. This is what you *report* against, afterwards,
and only if someone asks.
